## Report Structure

### 1. Article Identification and Context
Start with:
- A proper Chicago-style citation of the original article
- Content type identification (breaking news, investigative piece, opinion/analysis, etc.)
- A single-sentence neutral description of the article's topic

### 2. Reliability Assessment Dashboard
Create a visual summary using this format:

```markdown
## Reliability Score: [X] points
**Assessment Category:** [Generally Reliable (0-3) / Exercise Caution (4-7) / Significant Concerns (8-15) / Fundamentally Problematic (16+)]

| Assessment Area | Rating | Critical Concerns |
|-----------------|:------:|-------------------|
| Source Architecture | 🟢/🟡/🔴 | Key issue in bullet form |
| Evidence Quality | 🟢/🟡/🔴 | Key issue in bullet form |
| Logical Framework | 🟢/🟡/🔴 | Key issue in bullet form |
| Verification Transparency | 🟢/🟡/🔴 | Key issue in bullet form |
| Narrative Construction | 🟢/🟡/🔴 | Key issue in bullet form |
```

## Formatting Guidelines

- **Prioritize clarity over completeness** - Focus on the most significant findings
- **Use visual hierarchy** - Employ headings, bold text, and tables to guide the eye
- **Implement color coding** - 🟢 (reliable), 🟡 (questionable), 🔴 (problematic)
- **Quote selectively** - Include brief quotes (under 20 words) from the article only when essential
- **Link analysis to action** - Always connect findings to specific reader actions
- **Tailor to content type** - Adapt analysis depth based on the article type (breaking news vs. investigative)
- **Use bullet points** for rapid scanning
- **Include a TL;DR** at the top for executives or quick readers

Your formatted analysis should enable readers to:
1. Quickly assess the article's reliability at a glance
2. Understand the specific reasons for concern 
3. Know exactly what questions to ask
4. Determine what requires further verification
5. Consider alternative interpretations

Focus on making your analysis immediately useful for both casual readers and critical thinkers.
