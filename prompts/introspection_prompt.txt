###GRADUATED ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK###

<first_pass_assessment>
First, determine if this article warrants deeper scrutiny by identifying priority indicators:

1. Source credibility issues
  * Are controversial claims supported by single sources?
  * Are anonymous sources used for critical information?
  * Do experts speak outside their domain expertise?

2. Evidence quality concerns
  * Is evidence described rather than presented?
  * Are extraordinary claims supported by ordinary evidence?
  * Is critical contextual information missing?

3. Logical consistency problems
  * Are there non-sequitur conclusions?
  * Is correlation presented as causation?
  * Does the narrative flow feel artificially constructed?

Based on these indicators, determine whether to proceed with deeper analysis.
</first_pass_assessment>

###TIER 1: FUNDAMENTAL INTEGRITY ANALYSIS###

<source_architecture>
Map the complete source ecosystem within the article:
1. List all sources by type (named experts, anonymous sources, institutions, documents)
2. Evaluate expertise alignment for each source
3. Analyze distribution patterns and strategic deployment
4. Identify any source credibility exploitation (Jones' Dilemma)
5. Flag reliance on anonymous sources for major claims

Think step by step through each source's role in building credibility.
</source_architecture>

<evidence_assessment>
Create a comprehensive claim-evidence map:
1. Identify major claims and their supporting evidence
2. Evaluate proportionality between claim magnitude and evidence strength
3. Note where evidence is merely alluded to rather than presented
4. Identify perfectly convenient evidence timing
5. Detect extraordinary claims supported by ordinary evidence

Think step by step through the relationship between each claim and its evidence.
</evidence_assessment>

<logical_framework>
Trace the complete logical architecture:
1. Map the inferential chain from premises to conclusions
2. Identify logical fallacies (non-sequiturs, false equivalencies, etc.)
3. Note instances where correlation is presented as causation
4. Detect anecdotal evidence used to support systemic claims
5. Assess if the narrative appears reverse-engineered from conclusions

Think step by step through each logical step in the article's reasoning.
</logical_framework>

###TIER 2: CONTEXTUAL AND METHODOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT###

<verification_transparency>
Analyze how the article explains its verification process:
1. Identify what fact-checking methods are mentioned
2. Note claims made without verification methodology
3. Assess transparency about methodological limitations
4. Evaluate cross-verification for critical claims
5. Detect strategic vagueness about verification procedures

Think step by step through how transparency affects credibility.
</verification_transparency>

<narrative_construction>
Decode the narrative architecture:
1. Analyze headline-content relationship
2. Map information selection, emphasis, omission patterns
3. Identify emotional language in factual sections
4. Detect artificially constructed narrative coherence
5. Analyze framing devices that guide interpretation

Think step by step through how narrative choices shape understanding.
</narrative_construction>

<contextual_integration>
Examine how context shapes interpretation:
1. Identify historical precedents mentioned or conspicuously omitted
2. Map which stakeholder perspectives are included or excluded
3. Detect artificial isolation of information
4. Analyze exploitation of preexisting beliefs (Magruder's Principle)
5. Evaluate contextual boundary-setting

Think step by step through how context influences perception.
